## Introduction

### CS184

CS184 is a computer graphics class at Berkeley. This class covers how computers render images to the screen, add texture to 3D models, use shaders, etc. Our final project was VERY open ended, basically letting us do whatever we wanted as long as it had ***something*** to do with computer graphics. So of course, my friends and I took this opportunity to create a game.

<ProjectImage src={damagedGif} alt="Player getting damaged by a projectile" caption="Player getting damaged by a projectile" maxWidth="medium" />

We decided we wanted to make a top-down roguelike. This is a very popular genre of game, and for good reason. The key features of these include randomized levels, hard difficulty, no check points, collecting randomized items, and fighting randomized enemies. There's a lot of random-ness here to keep things unique, but... we did not do that. Adding this randomness just made it too difficult to meet our other requirements for this project! Just a basic game wouldn't be enough for a computer graphics class, so to supplement this, we went ***HEAVY*** with the shaders. We also added special lens mechanic, more on that later.

### Placeable lens

To get some more credit, we wanted to incorporate a lens. There was a large portion of the class dedicated to lenses and how they change the viewport. This makes a big difference on imaging for cameras, and therefore for computers as well. To incorporate this as a mechanic, we added placeable lense. Shooting projectiles through this lens refracts them at an angle, using a formula we derived in class! These refracted projectiles deal more damage to enemies, so it's in your best interest to try and use this!

### Boss fight

As already mentioned above, the level ends with a boss fight with a 3D teapot. This teapot was the main boss, because a previous project featured us applying shaders to a 3D teapot model, and it was a pain in the ass! So we wanted to get some revenge on that model. This boss fight is actually pretty hard. We didn't do a good job balancing here, we just made a bunch of projectile launching patterns for the teapot to rotate between. But it's possible to win!

## Conclusion

This project was a blast! I got to work with 2 close friends, and our final project was something that I can be proud to show off. It looks pretty cool! Gameplay-wise, however, there is a lot left to be desired. It's just too hard and not random enough! There's items, but they are preset at predetermined locations! Still, as an introduction into the world of GameDev and Godot for myself, I couldn't ask for anything better.
